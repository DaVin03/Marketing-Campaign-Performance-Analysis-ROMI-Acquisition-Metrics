# Marketing Campaign Performance Analysis

## Project Overview

In this project I analyze the performance of various marketing campaigns to evaluate their efficiency and return on marketing investment (ROMI).

The goal is to transform raw spending and traffic data into actionable metrics, identifying the most profitable channels, the best geographies for targeting, and user purchasing habits.

The analysis is structured to show an authentic analytical workflow: starting from basic metrics and tackling classic data aggregation pitfalls (such as the "average of averages" bias when calculating ROMI), moving toward advanced segmentation and volume normalization.

## Business Questions (Objectives)

The analysis answers the following strategic questions:

1. What is the overall ROMI and the specific ROMI for each campaign?
2. On which dates did I spend the most on advertising, and when did I generate the highest revenue?
3. When are conversion rates at their highest or lowest, and what is the average order value (AOV)?
4. When are buyers most active (Weekdays vs. Weekends)?
5. Which channels perform best (Social, Banner, Influencer, or Search)?
6. Which geographic areas are optimal for targeting (Tier 1 vs. Tier 2 cities)?

## Data Dictionary

The dataset I used contains daily logs for each campaign with the following information:

| **Column**        | **Description**                                          |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Date**          | Date the marketing budget was spent                      |
| **Campaign name** | Descriptive name of the campaign                         |
| **Category**      | Type of marketing source (e.g., social, search, banner)  |
| **Campaign id**   | Unique identifier for the campaign                       |
| **Impressions**   | Number of times the ad was shown                         |
| **Mark. budget**  | Budget spent on the campaign on that date (in INR)       |
| **Clicks**        | Number of visitors who clicked the ad                    |
| **Leads**         | Number of users who signed up and left their credentials |
| **Orders**        | Number of users who purchased the product                |
| **Revenue**       | Revenue generated by the campaign on that date           |

## Calculated Metrics (KPIs)

To evaluate campaign effectiveness, I transformed the raw data into the following metrics:

* **ROMI (Return on Marketing Investment):** `(Revenue - Marketing Spend) / Marketing Spend`
* **CTR (Click-Through Rate):** `Clicks / Impressions`
* **Conv. 1 (Visitor to Lead):** `Leads / Clicks`
* **Conv. 2 (Lead to Sale):** `Orders / Leads`
* **AOV (Average Order Value):** `Revenue / Orders`
* **CPC (Cost per Click):** `Marketing Spend / Clicks`
* **CPL (Cost per Lead):** `Marketing Spend / Leads`
* **CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost):** `Marketing Spend / Orders`
* **Gross Profit:** `Revenue - Marketing Spend`

## Methodology and Analytical Flow

### 1. Data Cleaning & Feature Engineering

I imported and cleaned the data, paying close attention to mathematical anomalies (e.g., divisions by zero on days with marketing spend but no clicks). Operational metrics like CPC and CTR were calculated on a daily basis.

### 2. The ROMI Bias (Analytical Correction)

The project highlights a common pitfall in data analysis: calculating daily ROMI row-by-row and then taking a simple average. I demonstrate how this approach mathematically distorts reality, subsequently implementing the correct calculation based on total aggregated costs and revenues per campaign.

### 3. Outlier Handling

I addressed anomalies on two fronts:

* **Technical Errors:** Removal of physically impossible records (e.g., number of clicks exceeding impressions, or conversion rates > 100%).
* **Top Performers:** Extreme revenue peaks were not discarded; instead, I isolated them into a dedicated subset to analyze "success cases" and understand which patterns to replicate.

### 4. Normalized Time Analysis

The weekday vs. weekend analysis goes beyond comparing absolute revenue volumes. I cross-reference the data with spend (CAC) and average conversion rates. This reveals not only *when we sell the most*, but *when customer acquisition costs are at their lowest*.

### 5. Segment Evaluation (Geo & Channel)

I grouped campaigns by macro-category and geographic target (Tier 1 vs. Tier 2), comparing Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) against ROMI to pinpoint the most scalable and efficient channels.
