# Marketing Campaign Performance Analysis

## Project Overview

In this project, I analyzed the performance of a few marketing campaigns to evaluate their efficiency and return on investment (ROMI). 

My goal was to take raw spending and traffic data and turn it into useful metrics to see which channels and geographic areas are actually working. I also wanted to show a realistic analytical workflow: starting with basic daily metrics, spotting and fixing a common aggregation mistake (averaging daily rates instead of totals), and then moving into data segmentation.

## Business Questions

This analysis tries to answer a few practical questions:

1. What is the actual ROMI for our campaigns?
2. On which dates did we spend the most, and when did we get the best revenue?
3. How do conversion rates and average order value (AOV) behave over time?
4. Are buyers more active or cheaper to acquire on weekends compared to weekdays?
5. Which channels perform better between Social, Banner, Influencer, and Search?
6. Does targeting Tier 1 cities work better than Tier 2?

## Data Dictionary

The dataset contains daily logs for each campaign:

| **Column**        | **Description** |
| :---              | :--- |
| **Date**          | Date the marketing budget was spent |
| **Campaign name** | Descriptive name of the campaign |
| **Category**      | Type of marketing source (e.g., social, search, banner) |
| **Campaign id**   | Unique identifier for the campaign |
| **Impressions**   | Number of times the ad was shown |
| **Mark. budget**  | Budget spent on the campaign on that date (in INR) |
| **Clicks**        | Number of visitors who clicked the ad |
| **Leads**         | Number of users who signed up and left their credentials |
| **Orders**        | Number of users who purchased the product |
| **Revenue**       | Revenue generated by the campaign on that date |

## Calculated Metrics (KPIs)

To evaluate the campaigns, I calculated the following metrics from the raw data:

* **ROMI:** `(Revenue - Marketing Spend) / Marketing Spend`
* **CTR (Click-Through Rate):** `Clicks / Impressions`
* **Conv. 1 (Visitor to Lead):** `Leads / Clicks`
* **Conv. 2 (Lead to Sale):** `Orders / Leads`
* **AOV (Average Order Value):** `Revenue / Orders`
* **CPC (Cost per Click):** `Marketing Spend / Clicks`
* **CPL (Cost per Lead):** `Marketing Spend / Leads`
* **CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost):** `Marketing Spend / Orders`

## Methodology and Analytical Flow

### 1. Data Cleaning
I imported and cleaned the dataset, handling some mathematical issues (like divisions by zero on days where we had budget spent but zero clicks). 

### 2. Fixing the ROMI Bias
A common mistake in marketing analysis is calculating the ROMI day by day and then just taking the average of that column. I showed how this mathematically distorts the results because it gives equal weight to days with very different budgets. To fix this, I calculated the final ROMI by aggregating the total costs and total revenues first.

### 3. Outlier Handling
I removed technical errors (like records showing more clicks than impressions). For days with unusually high revenue, I isolated them into a separate subset to see if there were specific patterns we could learn from, rather than just deleting them.

### 4. Normalized Time Analysis
Instead of just looking at total revenue on weekdays vs. weekends, I cross-referenced the days with the Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) and conversion rates to see when it's actually cheaper to acquire a user.

### 5. Segments Evaluation
Finally, I grouped the data by channel and geographic target (Tier 1 vs. Tier 2) to see where the budget is most efficient.

## Conclusion

Overall, the marketing campaigns generated a positive return during the analyzed period. However, looking at individual campaigns, the performance is pretty mixed.

Looking at the funnel, the visitor-to-lead conversion rate is quite low (around 1.3% - 2.8%), while the lead-to-sale rate is much better (9% - 15%). This might suggest that our main bottleneck is getting users to leave their contact info in the first place, so optimizing the landing page could be a good next step.

The Average Order Value (AOV) usually fluctuates between ₹5,000 and ₹6,000. While there are some peaks, it stays fairly consistent, so AOV alone doesn't give us a clear pattern to segment our customers right now. 

When comparing weekdays and weekends, there is an interesting difference in efficiency. Weekends tend to show a higher ROMI (above 0.4) compared to weekdays (0.3 - 0.4). The CAC also seems to drop a bit on weekends (₹3,000–₹4,000) compared to the ₹4,000 weekday baseline. Since AOV remains stable, running campaigns on weekends appears to be slightly more cost-effective.

Grouping the data by channel shows some distinct differences. The influencer campaigns performed very well, reaching a ROMI over 1.5. Search campaigns just barely broke even, and social campaigns actually showed a negative return. Based on this, it might be worth testing a shift in budget towards influencers to see if that performance holds up at a larger scale.

Finally, looking at geographic data, Tier 1 cities brought in a positive ROMI of about 0.35. Tier 2 cities performed poorly, with a ROMI below -0.2. It would probably make sense to focus our current budget on Tier 1 areas and pause Tier 2 spending until we figure out why it's not converting.
